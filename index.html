Thoughts on Progressive Enhancement

Progressive enhancement is a way of building websites that puts the main content first so everyone can see and use it, even if they have an older browser or a device that can’t handle fancy features. After the content is ready, you can add styles like colors and fonts, and extra interactive features like buttons or animations for people who can see them. This approach makes websites faster, easier to use, and works on phones, tablets, and computers. It also helps make websites more accessible to everyone. If you want to read more about it, check out this article: Progressive Enhancement by Matt Fantinel.

Mars Rover Project Revision and Reflection

In my rough draft, the website had main pages like Home, Rovers, FAQ, and Contact, with text and pictures. The FAQ was just normal paragraphs, the contact page didn’t have a working form, the images were not very clear, and the design was not well organized. In the final version, I made many improvements: I added an interactive FAQ so people can click on questions to see answers, created a table comparing the rovers with launch dates, landing dates, mission durations, and power sources, improved image clarity, added a working contact form, included meta tags for description and keywords, and added an email link in the footer. These changes made the website more clear, organized, and user-friendly.
